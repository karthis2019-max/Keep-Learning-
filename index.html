OHSA Injury Analysis Dashboard

An interactive Tableau dashboard analyzing workplace injury trends, safety incidents, and occupational health patterns from 2015–2025 using modern data visualization.

View Dashboard View GitHub Project

Methodology

The dashboard development process began with the preparation and validation of OSHA workplace injury and illness data spanning 2015–2025. The dataset was cleaned and standardized in Excel to ensure consistency across key variables, including incident classifications, industry sectors, geographic locations, and reporting periods. Data quality checks were performed to address missing values, remove inconsistencies, and improve the reliability of the analysis. Following data preparation, the dataset was imported into Tableau, where calculated fields, key performance indicators (KPIs), and custom measures were developed to support the analysis of workplace injury trends, severity patterns, and industry-specific risk factors. Exploratory data analysis was conducted to identify meaningful relationships and determine the metrics most relevant to occupational safety performance. The dashboard was designed using data visualization best practices, with a focus on clarity, usability, and interactivity. Dynamic filters, drill-down functionality, and interactive visualizations were incorporated to enable detailed exploration of injury trends across industries, occupations, locations, and time periods. The resulting dashboard provides a comprehensive view of workplace safety performance and supports data-driven decision-making through accessible and actionable insights.

3 Key Insights

1. Hand and finger injuries dominate severe incidents. Finger and fingertip injuries account for a significant proportion of severe workplace injuries.

2. Severe injuries are concentrated in specific states, particularly Texas, Florida, Pennsylvania and Ohio.

3. Hospitalization represents the majority of severe incident outcomes.

3 Recommendations

1. Strengthen machine guarding and PPE programs.

2. Increase preventive interventions within high-risk states and industries.

3. Expand proactive hazard identification and near-miss reporting programs.
